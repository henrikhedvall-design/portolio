@H2{Challenge} @BIGP{Oqto’s product had been designed by the people building it. They needed a design system clear enough to use on their own afterwards, so the product stays consistent as it grows. The design also had to meet contrast requirements, fix the most obvious UX problems and reflect Oqto’s brand, on the web and in the app.}
@STEP1{01 Foundation|Colours and typography|The system starts small. Sand, Mist, Orange and Ink carry the whole product and give it a look you don’t expect from an accounting platform. Sandor is the primary typeface, with Geist Mono in capitals for labels.}

@STEP{02 Design system|Components for a dense product|Buttons, fields, chips, menus and tables, each with its states. The Figma library and its written guidelines are connected to Claude Code, so new features follow the system from the start. Everything works in light and dark mode.}
@STEP{03 The product|Key screens, rebuilt|I redesigned a selection of screens chosen to cover as much of the product as possible, and reworked the flows that needed bigger changes. Below is one of them: building an automation in three steps – when, if and then.}
@STEP{04 The app|The same system in your pocket|The app covers what you need on the go: bank accounts, payments to sign, payroll and invoices, with the same components and tone as the web product.} @IMG{7|oqto-app.jpg|Eight screens from the Oqto mobile app on orange|2400|1500}
@H2{Outcome} @BIGP{Oqto now has a design system the team can keep building with on their own, and a product that looks and works the same on the web and in the app. It’s made for accounting firms, but feels as simple as a consumer app.}
